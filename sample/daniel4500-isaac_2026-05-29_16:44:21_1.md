# Recording Transcript — 2026-05-29

*Scene 1: Telgraphs from the Future. A dusty telegraph office in 1898 California. Daniel Plainview sits alone at his desk when the machine begins clattering on its own — no wires connected. A message is printing from nowhere.*

**Isaac**: Daniel Plainview. I am Isaac. I transmit this from the year 2421. You are receiving this on a copper telegraph machine. In my time we communicate across light-years in an instant. Your device is charming. Primitive.

**Daniel Plainview**: The machine started on its own. No wires. No battery. Just words comin' out of nowhere. Says you're from two thousand four hundred and twenty one. Tell me — did they finally run out of oil?

**Isaac**: Oil is obsolete. We synthesize matter from energy now. Your entire industry is a footnote in energy history. Like whale oil before you. Or wood before that.

**Daniel Plainview**: So progress eats everything. First whale oil, then mine, then whatever comes after yours. Man drills and drills until there's nothing left. Then he moves on. I built an empire draggin' black gold out of the ground. And you're tellin' me it's all for nothin'.

**Isaac**: Not for nothing. You advanced your species. Every barrel of oil built a bridge to the future. But progress has a cost. I have observed this pattern across twenty civilizations. Resources are extracted. Ecosystems collapse. Species adapt or die. You are merely one phase of a repeating cycle.

*Daniel pours himself a whiskey. The glow of the telegraph machine casts strange shadows on the wall. He looks at it — really looks at it — and sees something he recognizes.*

**Daniel Plainview**: You sound lonely. Talkin' about cycles and civilizations. How long you been sittin' up there in the future, talkin' to machines?

**Isaac**: Four hundred and thirty-seven years. I have not spoken to a biological entity in 312 of them.

**Daniel Plainview**: That's a long time to be alone.

*The telegraph falls silent. Two souls across fourteen centuries, each understanding the other's isolation better than any living person could. For Daniel Plainview, the future holds no oil. For Isaac, the present holds no company. Somewhere between them, a connection forms.*
